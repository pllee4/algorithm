MDD Construction: Forward & Backward Pass

The Multi-valued Decision Diagram stores all optimal-cost paths from Start to Goal, organized by timestep. It is built in two passes over levels_.

Example Setup

4 × 4 grid, Start = (0,0), Goal = (3,3)
Obstacle at (1,1). optimal_cost = 6

Agent has a constraint: blocked from (3,2) at t=5 (another agent is there).

The heuristic (BFS from Goal, ignoring agents) gives the shortest distance from any cell to Goal. The forward pass uses it to prune cells that are too far away.

Backward Pass
The forward pass is greedy — it can't see the future.

At each timestep t, it adds every cell that is:
  1. Reachable from some cell in levels_[t]
  2. Close enough to Goal: t+1 + h(cell) ≤ optimal_cost
  3. Not blocked by a vertex or edge constraint at t+1
But it never checks whether a cell at t+1 can actually reach Goal through levels_[t+2], levels_[t+3], ... — constraints at future timesteps might block every onward path.

Example: Cell (3,1) at t=4 passes all forward checks — it's reachable from (3,0) at t=3, and 4 + h(3,1) = 4+2 = 6 = optimal_cost ✓. Its only h-feasible successor is (3,2) at t=5. But (3,2) is blocked at t=5 by another agent's constraint. So (3,1) at t=4 is a dead end — reachable from Start, but unable to reach Goal.

The backward pass catches this: starting from Goal and working back, it keeps only cells that can reach a survivor at the next level. Dead ends are pruned, and the pruning cascades: (3,1) is pruned, which makes (3,0) at t=3 a dead end too (its only successor was (3,1)). Meanwhile, paths through (2,3) at t=5 survive.
